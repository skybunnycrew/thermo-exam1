Why the real loop is smaller

  1. Combustion takes time. 40–60° of crank angle, so it starts before TDC and finishes after. The sharp top corners round off and the peak pressure drops.
  2. Heat leaks to the walls. Compression and expansion follow n ≈ 1.3 instead of k = 1.4.
  3. Valves and ports don't switch at dead center. Intake closes after BDC, exhaust opens before BDC (blowdown), and the bottom-right corner disappears.
  4. Gas exchange costs work. A four-stroke pulls in below atmospheric and pushes out above it, adding a negative pumping loop. A throttled Otto engine pays the most.
  5. Real gases aren't cold air. c_p and c_v rise with temperature, which lowers peak temperature and pressure further. This model keeps them constant.
  6. Friction and blow-by don't show on the P-v diagram, but they take their share between indicated and brake power.

Beyond the numbers: who breathes the short-circuit?

In the crankcase-scavenged two-stroke (Otto, 2-stroke, Real), watch the green dots during scavenging: some fresh fuel–air mixture flows straight across the cylinder and out the exhaust port without burning. Small handheld equipment like backpack leaf blowers commonly uses this kind of engine.

California's Air Resources Board found that smog-forming emissions from small off-road engines already exceeded those from light-duty passenger cars in 2021, and that one hour of a commercial leaf blower emits as much smog-forming pollution as driving a car about 1,100 miles. The people standing next to these engines all day are landscaping crews. Compare the uniflow diesel two-stroke: its scavenging loses only air, never fuel.

Question for discussion: the engine is cheap, light, and simple, and those are real advantages. Who gets those benefits, and who pays the cost in what they breathe?

About the model. Ideal: cold-air-standard cycle, k = 1.4, P₁ = 1 bar, T₁ = 300 K, q_in = 1500 kJ/kg, r = 9 (Otto) or 17 (Diesel). Real: a single-zone model with a Wiebe heat-release curve (Otto: spark 20° before TDC, 55° burn; Diesel: injection 10° before TDC, a short premixed spike, then a 50° diffusion burn), polytropic n = 1.32 standing in for wall heat loss, 82% of the fuel energy reaching the gas, typical valve and port timing, and intake and exhaust pressures of 0.90 to 1.25 bar. The real curves are an illustrative model, not measured data. Gas temperature is shown only while the cylinder is closed. Keyboard: space plays or pauses, arrow keys step one sample.
